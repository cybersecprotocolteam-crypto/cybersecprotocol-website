The Map of Cybersecurity

Intro to Cybersecurity · about 5 minutes

1. One field, many specialities

Cybersecurity is not one skill. It is a family of specialities that work together, and this app follows that map, module by module:

  • Network Security: how data travels, and how to protect it on the way.
  • Ethical Hacking: attacking systems with permission, to find weaknesses first.
  • Defensive Security: hardening systems, detecting attacks and responding to incidents.
  • App & Mobile Security: how apps are broken, and how to build and use them safely.
  • Privacy & Data Protection: personal data, tracking and your rights.
  • Social Engineering: the human side, from phishing to manipulation.
  • Cryptography: the maths that keeps secrets and proves who is who.
  • OSINT: finding public information, ethically.
  • Cloud & IoT Security: the servers you rent and the devices you connect.
  • Careers: the jobs, the skills and the certificates.

2. The coding tracks

Security people read and write code every day. The coding tracks teach the languages you will meet most: Algorithms in C (thinking step by step), Python (automation and tools), Bash & Linux (the command line every server runs), JavaScript (the web, and attacks like XSS) and SQL (databases, and SQL injection).

Tip

You do not need to finish everything. Start with Network Security and one coding track, then follow what you enjoy most.

3. Red team, blue team

You will often hear two names. The red team plays the attacker, with permission, to find weaknesses. The blue team defends: it watches, detects and responds. Many people also work in GRC (governance, risk and compliance), writing the rules and checking that they are followed. All three are needed.

Key Takeaway

Pick a starting point, not a final destination. Every speciality uses the same basics: networks, operating systems, a little code and a careful mind.
