What Is Cybersecurity?

Intro to Cybersecurity · about 5 minutes

1. Protecting what matters

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting devices, networks, accounts and data from people who want to steal, change or break them. It covers your phone and your social media accounts as much as a bank's servers. Every message you send, every photo you keep and every payment you make depends on it.

Analogy

Think of your digital life as a house. Passwords are the locks, alerts are the alarm, and backups are the insurance. Cybersecurity is everything that keeps the house safe, and that helps you recover when something goes wrong.

2. The three goals

Security people judge every system by three goals, known as the CIA triad:

  • Confidentiality: only the right people can see the data.
  • Integrity: the data is not changed without permission.
  • Availability: the system works when you need it.

You will meet these three words in every module of this app.

3. Who attacks, and why

Most attackers are not movie hackers. They are criminals after money (stolen accounts, ransomware, scams), people collecting data to sell, and sometimes governments spying on each other. Most attacks start with something simple: a reused password, a fake message, or an app that was never updated.

Real-World

Many large breaches began with a single phishing email or one leaked password. That is good news: the habits you will learn here (strong, unique passwords, two-step verification, updates, thinking before you click) stop most real attacks.

Key Takeaway

Cybersecurity protects confidentiality, integrity and availability. Attacks usually exploit simple mistakes, so simple habits protect you. Next: the map of the field, and how the modules of this app fit into it.
